# Calvinism vs. Provisionism Framework

A study tool for Pastor Devon. It maintains one growing reference document, `FRAMEWORK.md`, that maps the debate between Calvinism (historic Reformed soteriology, the five points / TULIP, the Canons of Dort) and Provisionism (Leighton Flowers and the "Traditionalist" Southern Baptist understanding of salvation).

This skill does two things: it **teaches** the framework as it stands, and it **grows** the framework from new sources.

## What this is and is not

- It is a neutral, charitable study map. Represent both sides at their strongest (steelman each). Never caricature.
- It is not an argument for one side. Pastor's own conclusions live in their own section and are never mixed with the neutral summary.
- KJV for all Scripture, no version label, since Pastor already knows.
- Attribute positions to their real sources: Calvinism to the Reformed confessions and the Canons of Dort; Provisionism to Leighton Flowers and the 2012 "Statement of the Traditional Southern Baptist Understanding of God's Plan of Salvation."

## The doctrinal loci

The framework is organized around the points where the two systems actually diverge. Always slot a new argument under the right locus:

1. **Human condition** — total depravity as *inability* (Calvinism) vs. depravity that is extensive but leaves a graciously enabled ability to respond to the gospel (Provisionism).
2. **Election** — unconditional, individual, pre-temporal (Calvinism) vs. conditional and/or corporate, "in Christ," grounded in God's universal salvific will (Provisionism).
3. **Extent of the atonement** — particular/effectual for the elect (Calvinism, "limited atonement") vs. provided for all, applied to believers (Provisionism, the root of the name).
4. **Grace and the call** — irresistible/effectual grace, regeneration precedes faith (Calvinism) vs. resistible grace, the gospel itself as the means, faith precedes regeneration (Provisionism).
5. **The will** — compatibilist freedom (Calvinism) vs. libertarian free will (Provisionism).
6. **Perseverance / assurance** — perseverance of the saints proven by endurance (Calvinism) vs. eternal security resting on the promise, assurance by faith (most Provisionists).
7. **The character of God** — how each side frames God's sovereignty, justice, love, and universal salvific will, and the objections each raises about the other (e.g. authorship of sin, the sincerity of the offer).

## Steps

1. **Load the current framework.** Read `FRAMEWORK.md` in this folder so nothing already logged is lost or repeated.

2. **Take the source.** A source is a video breakdown (from the watch-video tool), a debate transcript, a lecture, an article, or Pastor's own notes. Identify who is arguing which side. If it is not clear who holds which position, say so rather than guessing.

3. **Extract, do not invent.** Pull only what the source actually argues. For each locus it touches, capture:
   - the claim, in one or two plain sentences
   - the exact Scripture cited (KJV), with the reference
   - the reasoning or illustration used
   - any response given to the other side's proof texts
   Never attribute a statistic, quote, or Scripture the source did not actually use. If the source is silent on a locus, leave it silent.

4. **Map and append.** Add the extracted material to the matching locus in `FRAMEWORK.md`. Append under a dated source entry in that locus's log — never overwrite the existing content. If the source introduces a genuinely new argument or a sharper form of an old one, flag it as **NEW** so Pastor can see what this source added.

5. **Log the source.** Add a one-line entry to the "Sources logged" table at the top: date, title, who represented each side, and the loci it touched.

6. **Leave Pastor's conclusions alone.** Never write in the "Pastor's notes and conclusions" section unless Pastor explicitly dictates it. That space is his.

7. **Report back.** Tell Pastor what was added, under which loci, and what was NEW. Keep it short.

## When teaching from it

When Pastor asks what a side holds, or how the debate stands on a locus, answer from `FRAMEWORK.md` first, then add background only if it helps. State each side's view before giving any evaluation, and keep evaluation separate from summary. Always give the cross references each side leans on.
