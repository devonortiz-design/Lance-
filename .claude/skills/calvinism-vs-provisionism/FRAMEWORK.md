# Calvinism vs. Provisionism — Living Framework

A growing study map. Each side is stated at its strongest. Neutral summary and Pastor's conclusions are kept separate. KJV throughout.

- **Calvinism** = historic Reformed soteriology (the five points / TULIP, the Canons of Dort, 1619).
- **Provisionism** = Leighton Flowers and the "Traditionalist" Southern Baptist view (the 2012 *Statement of the Traditional Southern Baptist Understanding of God's Plan of Salvation*). The name comes from the claim that Christ's death **provides** salvation for all, applied to those who believe.

## 1. Human condition (depravity and ability)

**Calvinism — Total Depravity (as inability).** Sin has so corrupted human nature that the unregenerate cannot understand, desire, or come to God on their own. The will is in bondage to sin until God frees it. Texts leaned on: 1 Corinthians 2:14 ("the natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God ... neither can he know them"), Romans 8:7-8, John 6:44 ("No man can come to me, except the Father which hath sent me draw him"), Ephesians 2:1 ("dead in trespasses and sins"), Jeremiah 13:23.

**Provisionism — depravity without total inability.** Humanity is truly and pervasively sinful, but not rendered unable to respond when God graciously enables through the gospel. The gospel call itself is the enabling means, so "dead" describes separation and condemnation, not the incapacity to believe when confronted with the Word. Texts leaned on: John 5:39-40 ("ye will not come to me"), Romans 1:16 ("the gospel ... is the power of God unto salvation"), Romans 10:17 ("faith cometh by hearing"), Acts 7:51 ("ye do always resist the Holy Ghost"), John 12:32.

## 2. Election

**Calvinism — Unconditional Election.** Before creation God chose specific individuals to save, not on the basis of foreseen faith or merit, purely of His good pleasure. Texts: Ephesians 1:4-5, Romans 9:11-16 (Jacob and Esau, "before the children ... had done any good or evil"), Acts 13:48 ("as many as were ordained to eternal life believed"), Romans 8:29-30 (the "golden chain").

**Provisionism — Conditional / corporate election, grounded in God's universal will.** God genuinely desires all to be saved and elects "in Christ" — election is corporate and/or conditioned on union with Christ by faith, not an arbitrary pre-selection of individuals. Texts: 1 Timothy 2:4 ("who will have all men to be saved"), 2 Peter 3:9 ("not willing that any should perish"), John 3:16, Ephesians 1:4 read as election *in Christ*, Romans 9 read as about corporate/national purpose and vessels of mercy vs. wrath, not individual salvation.

## 3. Extent of the atonement

**Calvinism — Limited (Particular) Atonement.** Christ died to actually secure the salvation of the elect, not merely to make salvation possible. The cross effectually accomplishes redemption for those given to the Son. Texts: John 10:11,15 ("I lay down my life for the sheep"), Ephesians 5:25 ("Christ ... loved the church, and gave himself for it"), Matthew 1:21, John 17:9 ("I pray not for the world").

**Provisionism — Unlimited (Provisional) Atonement.** Christ died for all, providing a real and sufficient sacrifice for every person; it is applied when a person believes. This is the doctrine the position is named for. Texts: 1 John 2:2 ("the propitiation ... for the sins of the whole world"), 1 Timothy 4:10 ("the Saviour of all men, specially of those that believe"), 2 Corinthians 5:14-15, John 3:16, Hebrews 2:9 ("taste death for every man"), 2 Peter 2:1 ("denying the Lord that bought them").

## 4. Grace and the call (and the order of faith and regeneration)

**Calvinism — Irresistible (Effectual) Grace; regeneration precedes faith.** The inward call of the Spirit to the elect cannot ultimately be refused; God first makes the dead sinner alive (regeneration), and the new heart then believes. Texts: John 6:37 ("All that the Father giveth me shall come to me"), John 6:44-45, Ezekiel 36:26-27, Philippians 1:29, Acts 16:14 (the Lord "opened" Lydia's heart).

**Provisionism — Resistible Grace; faith precedes regeneration.** Grace comes through the gospel and can be resisted; a person hears, is convicted, believes, and is then born again. The Word is the God-ordained means of grace, not an irresistible override. Texts: John 1:12-13, Acts 7:51, John 3:16 with 3:18, Ephesians 1:13 ("after that ye believed, ye were sealed"), Romans 10:9-14.

## 5. The will

**Calvinism — Compatibilism.** Human choices are genuinely willed yet certainly determined within God's sovereign decree; freedom means acting according to one's strongest desire, and apart from grace those desires are bound to sin.

**Provisionism — Libertarian free will.** For a choice to be morally responsible, the person must be able to do otherwise. God has granted a real ability to accept or reject the gospel call when graciously confronted with it.

## 6. Perseverance and assurance

**Calvinism — Perseverance of the Saints.** All whom God truly saves He keeps; they will persevere in faith to the end, and continuance is evidence of election. Texts: John 10:28-29, Philippians 1:6, Romans 8:38-39, 1 Peter 1:5.

**Provisionism — Eternal security by promise.** Most Provisionists (as Baptists) hold "once saved, always saved," but ground assurance in the promise of God received by faith rather than in perseverance as proof of election. Texts: John 5:24, John 10:28, Ephesians 1:13-14, 1 John 5:13 ("that ye may know that ye have eternal life").

_(Note: this is the locus of most overlap — both commonly affirm the believer's security; the difference is the basis of assurance.)_

## 7. The character of God (sovereignty, love, justice)

**Calvinism.** Magnifies God's sovereign freedom and grace: salvation is entirely of the Lord, boasting is excluded, and mercy is undeserved. Chief objection raised against Provisionism: it makes the decisive difference in salvation the sinner's choice, compromising grace and sovereignty.

**Provisionism.** Magnifies God's universal love, sincerity, and justice: God genuinely offers salvation to all and holds all responsible. Chief objection raised against Calvinism: unconditional election and limited atonement make God's universal offer insincere and risk making Him the author of the reprobate's damnation.

## Key shared battleground texts

Both sides claim these; log how each source handles them.

- **John 6:44** — divine drawing (inability vs. universal drawing, cf. John 12:32)
- **Romans 9** — Jacob/Esau, vessels of mercy and wrath (individual election vs. corporate/national purpose)
- **Ephesians 1:4-5** — chosen and predestinated (unconditional vs. "in Christ")
- **1 Timothy 2:4 / 2 Peter 3:9** — God's will that all be saved (revealed vs. decretive will; "all without exception" vs. "all kinds")
- **John 3:16** — "whosoever" and "the world"
- **Acts 13:48** — "ordained to eternal life"
- **1 John 2:2** — propitiation for "the whole world"
